\section{Introduction}

This document presents a simple mathematical example.

\begin{definition}
A function \(f\colon \RR \to \RR\) is called \emph{continuous} at
\(a \in \RR\) if, for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), there exists
\(\delta > 0\) such that
\[
  \abs{x-a}<\delta
  \implies
  \abs{f(x)-f(a)}<\varepsilon.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:example}
For every \(x \in \RR\),
\[
  x^2 + 1 > 0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(x^2 \geq 0\), we have
\[
  x^2+1 \geq 1 > 0.
\]
\end{proof}

By \Cref{thm:example}, the expression never vanishes.
